## 2. Editorial Quality & Human-Naturalness Pass

The five articles were evaluated against the core question: *If a real U.S. homeowner landed on one of these pages today, would it feel like a carefully edited reference handbook rather than AI-generated web filler?*

### Article 1: Water Heater Lifespan, Warning Signs & Replacement Guide
* **Strengths:** Clear, direct explanations of failure points, pressure vessel physics, and replacement cost variables.
* **Unnatural / AI-like Passages Found:** The technical description of sacrificial anode rods previously relied on textbook electrochemistry jargon without giving homeowners an intuitive visual reference.
* **Corrections Made:** Added a clear, plain-English homeowner analogy: *"Think of the anode rod as a sacrificial lightning rod for rust: as long as it remains intact, corrosive water chemistry dissolves the rod rather than the tank. Once the anode rod is fully depleted, dissolved oxygen and minerals attack the bare steel directly..."*
* **Remaining Observations:** Article reads crisply and authoritatively; perfectly balances homeowner maintenance with licensed trade safety boundaries.

### Article 2: Refrigerator Lifespan and Maintenance
* **Strengths:** Practical distinction between top-freezer mechanical simplicity and multi-door electronic complexity.
* **Unnatural / AI-like Passages Found:** The opening sentence of the technical section ("A refrigerator operates as a closed vapor-compression thermodynamic circuit...") sounded like a textbook syllabus rather than homeowner guidance. Also eliminated an overextended causal phrase in Factor 1 (*"suffer thermal motor breakdown"*).
* **Corrections Made:** Rewrote the technical opening to an intuitive, relatable explanation: *"Your refrigerator doesn’t actually generate cold—it removes heat. A sealed compressor circulates chemical refrigerant that absorbs heat from inside the fresh food and freezer compartments and expels it into your kitchen through the exterior condenser coils..."* Corrected Factor 1 to *"experience elevated compressor temperatures and increased run-time."*
* **Remaining Observations:** Natural, conversational yet technically disciplined tone.

### Article 3: HVAC Filter Replacement Schedule
* **Strengths:** Actionable month-by-month inspection guidance; clear distinction between 1-inch and 4-inch deep media.
* **Unnatural / AI-like Passages Found:** Discussion of "external static pressure" and "CFM" was abstract for non-technical readers.
* **Corrections Made:** Added a relatable everyday analogy: *"Think of static pressure like sipping through a straw: drinking water through a wide straw takes almost no effort, but trying to pull a thick milkshake through a crushed straw takes immense suction. When an air filter is caked in dust or overly restrictive, your furnace blower faces that same high resistance, reducing airflow (measured in Cubic Feet per Minute, or CFM)..."*
* **Remaining Observations:** Completely avoids the simplistic "higher MERV = bad" trap while empowering homeowners with clear inspection heuristics.

### Article 4: Major Home Components Lifespan Reference
* **Strengths:** Comprehensive master reference table covering structural envelope, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and appliance assets.
* **Unnatural / AI-like Passages Found:** Academic introductory language (*"Every physical system in a residential structure is subject to mechanical entropy, chemical oxidation, or thermal fatigue..."*).
* **Corrections Made:** Grounded the introduction in real homeowner experience: *"Every major system in a home is slowly wearing down from heat cycles, friction, water exposure, or rust. For most homeowners and home buyers, the challenge isn’t that things age—it’s that they age out of sight. A water heater quietly makes hot water for ten years, until suddenly a gallon of water a minute is pooling on the basement floor..."*
* **Remaining Observations:** Framing component lifespans as practical capital budgeting milestones rather than emergency deadlines relieves homeowner anxiety and promotes proactive budgeting.

### Article 5: How to Find and Use Your Home's Main Water Shutoff
* **Strengths:** Calm, clear, step-by-step guidance for an urgent emergency situation.
* **Unnatural / AI-like Passages Found:** The previous Quick Answer started with broad geographical framing rather than addressing the immediate emergency need of a homeowner with water gushing across a floor.
* **Corrections Made:** Rewrote the Quick Answer to immediate, action-oriented instructions: *"If water is flooding your home right now, head immediately to the interior perimeter wall on the side of the house facing the street—most commonly in the basement near the front wall, in a crawlspace, or near the water heater on a concrete slab. Look for a brass valve where the primary service pipe enters through the floor or wall. Turn a straight lever handle 90 degrees so it crosses the pipe, or turn a round wheel handle clockwise until it stops..."*
* **Remaining Observations:** Highly reassuring, practical, and clear.

* **Tier A (Documented Standard / Statutory Rule):** Framed firmly using official source citations (e.g. NFPA 72 10-year smoke alarm replacement, CPSC 120°F scald baseline, FDA ≤40°F refrigerator temperature).

## 6. Safety & Professional Boundaries Review

All procedures were verified against the four-tier safety model:
1. **Tier 1 (Safe Homeowner Observation):** Dollar-bill gasket test, monthly filter light test, visual corrosion checks, shutoff valve location.
2. **Tier 2 (Routine Homeowner Maintenance):** Annual sediment draining via garden hose, basic filter replacement, valve exercising.
3. **Tier 3 (Professional Diagnosis / Skilled Homeowner):** Sacrificial anode rod loosening (with caution regarding breaker bar torque on aging tank threads), high-limit furnace trip diagnostics.
4. **Tier 4 (Licensed Trade Work):** Gas line servicing, refrigerant recovery, main electrical panel replacement, and pressurized vessel replacement. Qualified with: *"Local municipal building codes and licensing requirements vary; always verify requirements with your local building inspection department."*
